  <div className="story">
    <div>
      I landed at Haneda around 4:50 p.m., which left just enough time to get
      into the city, check into a hotel near Tokyo Station, and find dinner.
    </div>
    <div>
      After a flight and the usual airport-to-hotel logistics, I didn't want to
      attempt a packed first evening. Dinner at
      <strong>Tsujita Nihonbashiyaesu</strong> was a good place to start: tsukemen,
      or dipping ramen, with the noodles served separately from the concentrated
      broth.
    </div>
    <div>
      The ordering process was part of the experience: buy your meal from the
      vending machine outside, wait for your turn to be called, then head in and
      get served. It was a great first meal and a satisfying way to ease back
      into Japan.
    </div>
  </div>

<section className="chapter" id="tokyo-wandering">
  <div className="chapter-kicker">December 17 · Day 2</div>
  <h2>Tokyo: no plans, just wandering</h2>
  <div className="photo-grid photo-grid--pair">
    <figure className="photo-slot">
      <img
        src="/assets/img/2024-12-japan/02-tokyo-brunch.jpg"
        alt="Toro and uni bowls"
      />
      <figcaption className="photo-caption">
        Brunch at Tsukiji Itadori Unitora.
      </figcaption>
    </figure>
    <figure className="photo-slot">
      <img
        src="/assets/img/2024-12-japan/02-tokyo-tonkatsu.jpg"
        alt="Tonkatsu dinner"
      />
    </figure>
  </div>
  <div className="story">
    <div>
      The next day was deliberately more relaxed. We had food stops and shopping
      destinations in mind, but no ambitious sightseeing route to follow.
    </div>
    <div>
      We started with seafood bowls at <strong>Tsukiji Itadori Unitora</strong>,
      where the toro and uni were the main draw. Food was a big part of how we
      planned this trip: rather than trying to visit every landmark, we made
      room for restaurants and specific dishes we'd wanted to try.
    </div>
    <div>
      From there, we headed to <strong>Nakano Broadway</strong> for a friend who
      loves watches. The complex is known for its many small specialty shops,
      and it is the sort of place where browsing is part of the activity. You
      can go in looking for something specific and still end up spending time
      looking at things you hadn't planned on.
    </div>
    <div>
      We also made the obligatory stops at Don Quijote, MUJI, and Uniqlo. These
      aren't exactly undiscovered destinations, but I always enjoy checking out
      these stores and stocking up on resupplies.
    </div>
    <div>
      Dinner was tonkatsu at <strong>Tonkatsu Aoki</strong>, another meal worth
      building the day around.
    </div>
    <div>
      It was a simple day by design: eat, shop, wander, eat again. We weren't
      trying to maximize the number of neighborhoods visited or race across the
      city for one more photo. The relaxed pace was short-lived, though. That
      evening, we needed to make our way toward Hiroshima.
    </div>
  </div>
  <aside className="journal-note">
    <h3>Tokyo to Hiroshima: the overnight work shift</h3>
    <div>
      The train ride also doubled as the start of my attempt to keep up with
      work while traveling.
    </div>
    <div>
      My working hours followed the U.S. East Coast, which meant evenings and
      early mornings in Japan weren't necessarily free time. Instead of
      finishing sightseeing and having the rest of the night to myself, I often
      had to switch from traveling to working.
    </div>
    <div>
      The schedule required some compromises. There were trains to catch, hotel
      check-ins to coordinate, and the usual need to get enough sleep to
      function the next day. I tried to fit work into the available windows, but
      that didn't always leave much room for rest.
    </div>
    <div>
      It's one thing to look at a time-zone conversion when planning a trip.
      It's another to actually live it while moving between cities. A day that
      looks perfectly reasonable on an itinerary can feel very different when it
      ends with a work shift.
    </div>
  </aside>
</section>

<section className="chapter" id="hiroshima">
  <div className="chapter-kicker">December 19 · Day 4</div>
  <h2>Hiroshima: a history that deserves your time</h2>
  <div className="story">
    <div>

      <strong>Peace Memorial Park</strong> and
      <strong>Peace Memorial Museum</strong> aren't places I would want to rush
      through just to stay on schedule. I am the kind of museum visitor who
      reads every plaque, while my friends tend to move faster—so I inevitably
      lagged behind and had to catch up.
    </div>
    <div>
      The museum was fascinating and deeply sad. Seeing the history and the
      remains firsthand made it feel more immediate than reading about it from
      afar. It was a somber visit, but an important one; some places deserve the
      time and attention to understand what happened there.
    </div>
  </div>

      futons. We arrived so late that I skipped dinner and went straight into work
      mode again.
