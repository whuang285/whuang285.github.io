    <h3>Choosing flights to Switzerland</h3>
    <div>
      For a winter trip centered on Zermatt, I compared flights into Zurich with
      alternatives through Geneva and Milan. The cheapest flight wasn't
      necessarily the best deal once I factored in the additional ground
      transportation, travel time, and convenience of getting to my first stop.
    </div>
    <div>
      I also compared paying cash with redeeming points. For award flights, I
      looked beyond the mileage price to include taxes and fees, any transfer
      bonuses available at the time, and the cash fare I would otherwise have
      paid. I also checked the fare rules carefully: a cheaper basic-economy
      ticket could become less attractive if it restricted checked baggage or
      advance seat selection, particularly when traveling with snowboarding
      equipment.
    </div>
    <div>
      Ultimately, I chose the option that offered the best overall value for the
      trip, rather than optimizing for the lowest advertised airfare alone.

    <h3>Using SBB Mobile and EasyRide</h3>
    <div>
      The <a href="https://www.sbb.ch/en">SBB Mobile app</a> made it much easier
      to navigate Switzerland's public transportation. We used it to check train
      connections and figure out how to get between destinations. Instead of
      buying every ticket at a station, we could manage much of the process on
      our phones.
    </div>
    <div>
      One particularly useful feature is EasyRide. For eligible journeys, you
      check in on the app before boarding, travel as usual, and check out when
      you arrive. The app records the journey and calculates the cheapest fare,
      so you don't have to select a point-to-point ticket every time.
    </div>
    <div>
      Before using EasyRide, make sure your payment method is set up and your
      Half Fare Card is correctly registered in the app.
    </div>
    <div className="tip">
      <strong>Tip:</strong> Check in before boarding and wait for confirmation
      that EasyRide is active. Compare fares before traveling, too: some Saver
      tickets and other offers need to be purchased separately and may be
      cheaper than the fare EasyRide calculates.
    </div>
    <div>
      For us, the combination of the Half Fare Card, individual tickets, and SBB
      Mobile gave us a good balance of savings and flexibility. We could plan
      our longer journeys while keeping the ticketing process simple along the
      way.

    <div className="eyebrow">First stop · Jan 18–23</div>
    <h2>Zermatt: snowboarding and the Matterhorn</h2>
    <div>
      Zermatt was the anchor of the trip because I wanted several days on the
      mountain. I arrived through Visp and stayed at Hotel Matterhornblick. The
      plan was to spend multiple days snowboarding, with a rest day in between
      rather than trying to pack sightseeing into every afternoon.
    </div>
    <div>
      One option on the list was crossing over to the Cervinia side in Italy.
      Other non-snowboarding options included the Gornergrat railway, Matterhorn
      Glacier Paradise, walking around the village, or fitting in a short hike
      if conditions allowed. I would decide on the mountain excursions based on
      visibility and energy instead of locking every day in too early.
    </div>
    {/* prettier-ignore */}
    <div>
      We spent two days snowboarding, took a rest day to explore Zermatt, then
      returned to the slopes for another two days. This gave us a chance to
      recharge without cutting our snowboarding time short. On one of those
      days, we even crossed over to the Italian side of the mountain for a
      change of scenery. If you plan to do the same, keep an eye on lift
      operating hours, weather conditions, and your route back to Switzerland.
      <strong> You don't want to get stranded on the Italian side after the 
      lifts close!</strong>
    </div>
    <div className="photo-grid">
      <figure className="media">
        <img
          src="/assets/img/2026-01-switzerland/zermatt-matterhorn.jpg"
          alt="Matterhorn view"
        />
        <figcaption>The classic Zermatt view.</figcaption>
      </figure>
    </div>
    <div className="tip">
      <strong>Planning tip:</strong> If snowboarding is a priority, give Zermatt
      a few days. A multi-day stay gives you more flexibility to pick the best
      weather and visibility conditions, rather than having your entire
      experience depend on a single day. We also found it helpful to focus on
      different areas of the mountain each day instead of trying to cover the
      entire resort every time.
    </div>
    <div>
      When we left Zermatt to continue the rest of our trip, we used SBB’s
      luggage shipping service to send our snowboard equipment directly to
      Zurich Airport. There was an SBB luggage drop-off point right at Zermatt
      train station and a pickup point at the airport, which made the whole
      process incredibly convenient. It meant we could travel between
      destinations without hauling our snowboard gear and pick it up when we
      were ready to fly home. I’d recommend this if you have at least 3–5 days
      before you need your luggage at the destination, giving it some buffer in
      case of winter weather delays.

    <div className="eyebrow">Second stop · Jan 23–27</div>
    <h2>Interlaken and the Jungfrau region</h2>
    <div>
      IInterlaken was our base for exploring the surrounding mountains and
      villages rather than a destination where we planned to spend every day in
      town. From here, we wanted to visit Jungfraujoch, Grindelwald First and
      the Cliff Walk, Iseltwald and its lakeside Piano Dock, Giessbach Falls,
      Lauterbrunnen, Mürren, and possibly Schilthorn if the weather cooperated.
    </div>
    <div>
      With so many places to choose from, the biggest challenge was deciding
      which excursions were worth prioritizing and how much we could
      realistically fit into each day. Winter weather and seasonal operating
      schedules also meant we couldn't treat every stop as a guaranteed part of
      the itinerary.
    </div>
    <h3>Jungfraujoch and Grindelwald</h3>
    <div>
      We originally planned to visit Jungfraujoch in the morning and head to
      Grindelwald First afterward if we had enough time. It was an ambitious
      combination, but we decided to give it a try.
    </div>
    <div>
      Fortunately, we managed to visit both on the same day, including the Cliff
      Walk at Grindelwald First. The trade-off was that Grindelwald felt a
      little rushed. We still made it down before the lifts closed, but there
      wasn't much room to linger.
    </div>

      Looking back, combining the two was possible with an early start and
      careful attention to the day's transport and lift schedules. However, I
      wouldn't plan the day around having plenty of time at both places. If
      either destination is a priority, consider giving it a full day instead of
      trying to squeeze in another mountain excursion.
    </div>
    <div>
      One thing I hadn't fully appreciated when planning was that Grindelwald
      First is also a ski area. It would have been fun to snowboard there, too,
      but we prioritized seeing the sights this time. If you're visiting to ski
      or snowboard, you may want to dedicate a separate day to the slopes rather
      than combine it with Jungfraujoch.
    </div>
    {/* prettier-ignore */}
    <div className="tip">
      <strong>Planning tip:</strong> We checked the mountain webcams each
      morning before deciding where to go. There are several useful resources,
      including the <a href="https://www.jungfrau.ch/en-gb/live/webcams/">
      official Jungfrau Region webcams</a> and
      <a href="https://en.map.grindelwald.swiss/live.html">Grindelwald
      Tourism’s live conditions page</a>. Check cameras at the specific
      elevations you plan to visit, since conditions in the valley can be
      very different from those at the mountain peaks.
    </div>
    <h3>Iseltwald and Giessbach Falls</h3>
    <div>
      Iseltwald was on the list for the lakeside walk and the famous Piano Dock.
      I also planned to visit Giessbach Falls nearby. The funicular was noted as
      not operating for the planned visit, so check seasonal operations rather
      than assuming every lift or funicular will be running in winter.
    </div>
    <div>
      We ended up visiting Iseltwald and then spending the rest of the day
      exploring Interlaken. One thing to know before visiting Iseltwald is that
      access to the Piano Dock is restricted by a turnstile, and you'll need to
      pay an entrance fee to step onto the dock. When we visited during the
      off-season, there was no staff member at the entrance, and we were the
      only people on the dock. However, I've heard that staff may be present at
      other times. Visiting off-peak made for a much quieter experience,
      especially at a spot that's become popular from social media.
    </div>
    <h3>Lauterbrunnen, Mürren, and Schilthorn</h3>
    <div>
      Another day was set aside for Lauterbrunnen's valley and Staubbach Falls,
      followed by Mürren and Schilthorn/Piz Gloria if visibility was good. These
      places are close enough to group geographically, but mountain transport
      and winter conditions still determine how much you can realistically do.

    <div className="eyebrow">Third stop · Jan 27–30</div>
    <h2>Lucerne and the surrounding mountains</h2>
    <div>
      Lucerne was the next base, with time for the old town and Chapel Bridge as
      well as a possible mountain excursion. Pilatus and Rigi were options, and
      the wider plan also included Mount Titlis and Engelberg, including the
      Cliff Walk and Glacier Cave.
    </div>
    <div>
      Pilatus, Rigi, and Titlis were all on our list, but we didn't treat them
      as interchangeable checklist items. Since the peaks sit at different
      elevations, visibility and weather conditions can vary significantly
      between them. Unfortunately, we weren't very lucky with the weather during
      our stay in Lucerne. It was cloudy and drizzling throughout our visit, and
      after checking the webcams each day, we decided to skip all three
      mountains. With visibility that poor, we would have likely seen little
      more than a wall of white.
    </div>
    <div>
      Instead, we took our time exploring Lucerne itself. We visited the Chapel
      Bridge, wandered through the Old Town, and stopped by the Lion Monument.
      On our second day, we bought a museum pass and visited a few museums that
      hadn't originally been part of our itinerary. It turned out to be a great
      alternative to the mountain excursions we'd missed.
    </div>
    <div>
      The Swiss Museum of Transport was a highlight. It's huge, with so much to
      explore that you could easily spend several hours there. We also visited
      the Rosengart Collection and the History Museum. But the biggest surprise
      for me was Gameorama, a museum dedicated to games. As someone who loves
      playing games, I had a great time trying out everything from arcade
      machines to games from different eras. I could easily have spent hours
      there, but we only had about two hours to spare.
    </div>
    <div className="tip">
      <strong>Planning tip:</strong> Don't plan your entire Lucerne itinerary
      around a mountain excursion. Check the webcams at different elevations
      each morning, since conditions can vary widely. Having a few museums or
      indoor activities as backup options makes it much easier to adapt to bad
      weather without feeling like you've lost a day.
    </div>

      airport stressful. If Rhine Falls is on your list, check the winter
      visitor arrangements and train schedules before deciding whether it fits
      your itinerary.

  <section className="section" id="winter-tips">
    <div className="eyebrow">Things I'd plan around</div>
    <h2>Winter planning tips</h2>
    <ul className="checklist">
      <li>
        <strong>Check mountain webcams before heading out.</strong> Visibility
        can vary significantly between elevations, so check cameras for the
        specific peaks you plan to visit each morning. We skipped all our
        planned mountain excursions around Lucerne because the weather stayed
        cloudy and drizzly.
      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Have a bad-weather backup plan.</strong> Keep a few indoor
        activities in mind for days when mountain conditions aren't worth the
        trip. Our unexpected museum visits in Lucerne, especially Gameorama,
        turned out to be some of the highlights of our stay.
      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Group stops by region.</strong> Use Interlaken as a base for
        nearby valleys and villages rather than crisscrossing the region.
      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Check seasonal operations.</strong> Cable cars, funiculars,
        trails, and attractions may have winter schedules or maintenance
        closures. Verify operating hours before building your day around a
        particular excursion.
      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Compare pass coverage carefully.</strong> Include mountain
        railways and excursion discounts in your calculations, not just
        intercity trains. We compared the Swiss Travel Pass against individual
        tickets with the Swiss Half Fare Card and found the Half Fare option
        worked out best for our itinerary.
      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Plan mountain days around the forecast.</strong> If snowboarding
        is a priority, give Zermatt multiple days so you have more flexibility
        with weather and visibility. At larger resorts, focusing on different
        areas of the mountain each day can also make your time more manageable.
      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Consider shipping bulky luggage ahead.</strong> We used SBB's
        luggage shipping service to send our snowboard equipment from Zermatt to
        Zurich Airport, which made traveling between destinations much easier.
