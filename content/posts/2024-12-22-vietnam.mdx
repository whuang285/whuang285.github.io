<details>
  <summary>Planning the trip</summary>
  <h3>Where to stay</h3>
  <div className="story">
    <div>
      Accommodation took a surprising amount of coordination. We had to consider
      not just location and price, but also how many people each place could
      sleep, whether everyone’s arrival and departure times lined up, and
      whether we could leave our luggage somewhere when we checked out.
    </div>
    <div>
      Our Vietnamese friends actually recommended Airbnb over hotels, and it
      worked well for a group trip like this. Having a shared space made it
      easier for everyone to stay together, especially with different groups
      arriving and leaving throughout the trip.
    </div>
    <div>
      In Hanoi, we stayed in the <strong>Old Quarter</strong>, which I’d
      recommend for a first visit. It puts you close to Hoan Kiem Lake,
      restaurants, street food, and plenty of places to explore on foot. The
      area around Hang Gai, Hang Trong, and the northern side of Hoan Kiem Lake
      is worth looking at if you want to be central without staying right in the
      middle of the busiest nightlife streets. The trade-off is that the Old
      Quarter can be noisy, so check reviews and ask about street noise before
      booking.
    </div>
    <div>
      In Hue, we tried to stay near the city center so we could get around
      easily and access the main sights without spending too much time traveling
      between them.
    </div>
    <div>
      For a group, I’d also check the luggage arrangements directly with the
      host. Don’t assume that being able to check in early or leave bags after
      checkout is included.
    </div>
  </div>
  <h3>Getting the Vietnam visa</h3>
  <div className="story">
    <div>
      The visa application process was one of the less reassuring parts of
      planning. The website felt rudimentary, and we ran into issues where pages
      failed to load or applications wouldn’t submit. Some friends had to try
      multiple times using different browsers or devices.
    </div>
    <div>
      My recommendation is to apply through the official government portal,
      double-check every passport detail before submitting, and leave enough
      time to deal with technical issues or processing delays. Save a digital
      copy of the approved visa and print a copy to carry with your travel
      documents. The official portal and fees can change, so check the current
      instructions before your own trip.
    </div>
    <div>Official portal: https://evisa.gov.vn/</div>
  </div>
  <h3>Cash and getting money</h3>
  <div className="story">
    <div>
      I was able to withdraw cash in Vietnam using my Charles Schwab debit card.
      If you have the card, this is another reason I recommend it for traveling:
      Schwab reimburses ATM operator fees worldwide, so I didn't have to worry
      about finding an ATM with the lowest withdrawal fees. And, always remember
      to choose being charged in the <strong>local currency</strong> to avoid
      currency-conversion markup offered by the ATM.
    </div>
    <div>
      Cash was important throughout the trip. Some places accepted cards, but
      others preferred cash or local QR-code payment systems. As foreign
      visitors, we generally couldn’t rely on the local QR payment options, so
      having Vietnamese Dong on hand made things much easier.
    </div>
    <div>
      I’d recommend withdrawing some cash when you arrive and keeping smaller
      denominations available for everyday purchases. You don’t need to carry
      all your money at once, but you also don’t want to find yourself unable to
      pay because a shop doesn’t accept international cards.
    </div>
  </div>
</details>

<section className="chapter" id="hanoi">
  <div className="chapter-kicker">December 22–26, 2024</div>
  <h2>Hanoi — the wedding and exploring the Old Quarter</h2>
  <div className="story">
    <div>
      We arrived in Hanoi on December 22 after flying in from Osaka. After
      making our way into the city, we checked into our Airbnb in the Old
      Quarter. This was where we’d be based for the first part of the trip, with
      friends arriving at different times and the wedding coming up.
    </div>
    <div>
      The Old Quarter was a convenient place to stay for a first visit to Hanoi.
      There were plenty of places to eat and grab coffee, and it was easy to
      spend time wandering around without needing to plan every stop in advance.
    </div>
    <h3>A day exploring Hanoi</h3>
    <div>
      December 23 was our day to explore Hanoi before the wedding festivities
      began. We had a pretty casual itinerary, with a mix of food stops and
      wandering around the Old Quarter.
    </div>
    <div>
      Looking back, we stopped at Phở Lý Quốc Sư, Bún Cá Hạnh Bồ, and Bún Chả
      Sinh Từ, along with a few cafés: Hanoi 1990s Cafe and Thaiyen Cafe. We
      also made time for Suzi Spa. There was no need to squeeze every major
      attraction into one day, especially with the wedding taking up the
      following day.
    </div>
    <div>
      One of the nice things about traveling with Vietnamese friends was that we
      didn’t have to figure everything out from scratch. We could leave room for
      recommendations and follow their lead instead of spending the whole trip
      checking maps and reviews.
    </div>
    <h3>The wedding</h3>
    <div>
      December 24 was the main event of the trip: our friends’ wedding in Hanoi.
    </div>
    <div>
      The day started early, with different groups heading to the homes of the
      bride and groom for the traditional ceremonies. The morning included the
      Ăn Hỏi, the Vietnamese engagement ceremony, followed by the Đón Dâu, the
      bride-receiving ceremony. After the morning festivities and lunch, we made
      our way to the venue for the vows ceremony and reception dinner at the
      National Convention Center.
    </div>
    <div>
      It was a full day, and a very different experience from attending a
      wedding back home. There were multiple parts to the celebration, family
      traditions, and plenty of coordination involved in getting everyone to the
      right place at the right time.
    </div>
    <div>
      The wedding was the reason we planned the trip in the first place, so it
      was nice to finally be there in person and celebrate with our friends.
    </div>
    <h3>A slower day after the wedding</h3>
    <div>
      After the wedding, we had a more relaxed day on December 25. We went to
      Tầm Vị for lunch, followed by Phe La. It was a good change of pace after
      the early start and long day before.
    </div>
    <div>
      We had originally put together a longer list of Hanoi sights, including
      Hoan Kiem Lake, Ngoc Son Temple, the Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum, the One Pillar
      Pagoda, and the Temple of Literature. But with the wedding and time spent
      catching up with friends, we didn't need to treat the itinerary as a
      checklist. There were plenty of reasons to leave some things for another
      visit.
    </div>
    <h3>Ha Long Bay day trip</h3>
    <div>
      On December 26, we took a day trip to Ha Long Bay. We were picked up in
      the morning and headed to Tuan Chau International Marina, where we boarded
      the tour boat.
    </div>
    <div>
      The itinerary included a buffet lunch, Sung Sot Cave, Luon Cave, and Ti
      Top Island. At Luon Cave, there was an option to explore the water by
      kayak. After the stops, we headed back to the harbor and took the bus back
      to Hanoi, arriving in the Old Quarter around 9 PM.
    </div>
    <div>
      It was a long day, with several stops packed into one excursion, but it
      gave us a chance to see the limestone islands and scenery that Ha Long Bay
      is known for without having to organize the transportation ourselves. If
      you're visiting Hanoi and want to see the bay but don't have time for an
      overnight cruise, a day trip is one option to consider. Just keep in mind
      that you'll spend a fair amount of the day traveling to and from the bay.
    </div>
  </div>
</section>
<section className="chapter" id="hue">
  <div className="chapter-kicker">December 27–29, 2024</div>
  <h2>Huế — imperial history in the rain</h2>
  <div className="story">
    <div>
      On December 27, we flew from Hanoi to Huế. We checked into our Airbnb in
      the afternoon and had a little time to settle in before heading out for
      food. We stopped at Han Cafe and Bánh Khoái Hồng Mai, getting our first
      taste of the city.
    </div>
    <div>
      Huế was the part of the trip where we focused more on Vietnam's imperial
      history. Our original list included the Imperial City, the royal tombs,
      and a boat tour on the Perfume River. We managed to fit in the major
      sights, although the weather wasn't always on our side.
    </div>
    <h3>Exploring the Imperial City in the rain</h3>
    <div>
      It rained during our time in Huế, which wasn't ideal for sightseeing. We
      visited the Imperial City wearing ponchos, trying to make the most of the
      day despite the weather.
    </div>
    <div>
      The Imperial City is part of the Complex of Huế Monuments, a UNESCO World
      Heritage site. Inside the citadel are the remains of the former imperial
      capital, including palaces, courtyards, and the Forbidden Purple City,
      which was once reserved for the emperor and the royal household.
    </div>
    <div>
      It would have been nicer to explore in dry weather, but we still got to
      see the historic complex. If you're planning a visit, it's worth bringing
      a rain jacket or poncho, especially during the wetter months. Huế is known
      for its rainy weather, and ours was a reminder that the forecast can have
      a real impact on sightseeing.
    </div>
    <div>
      That evening, we took a boat tour on the Perfume River. After spending the
      day walking around historic buildings in the rain, being on the river was
      a different way to experience the city. We also had Bún Bò Huế and stopped
      for Bánh Mì Trường Tiền O Thọ during our time here.
    </div>
    <h3>A day visiting the royal tombs</h3>
    <div>
      On December 29, we spent the day visiting three of Huế's royal tombs. The
      three major tombs commonly visited on this route are Minh Mạng, Khải Định,
      and Tự Đức.
    </div>
    <div>
      These aren't all in the same place as the Imperial City, so visiting them
      involves traveling out into the surrounding countryside. Having
      transportation arranged makes it much easier to see several in one day
      rather than trying to organize a separate ride for each stop.
    </div>
    <div>
      If you're planning the same day trip, a commonly recommended route is
      **Minh Mạng → Khải Định → Tự Đức**, although the best order depends on
      where you're starting and how you're getting around. The tombs each have a
      different architectural style: Minh Mạng is known for its formal layout
      and landscaped grounds, Khải Định for its elaborate decoration and mix of
      Vietnamese and European influences, and Tự Đức for its gardens and
      tranquil setting.
    </div>
    <div>
      We also stopped for food throughout the day, including Bánh Ép Trang and
      Cơm Niêu Chân. By the end of our time in Huế, we'd covered both the
      imperial sites inside the city and the tombs outside it.
    </div>
  </div>
</section>
<section className="chapter" id="hoian">
  <div className="chapter-kicker">December 30, 2024</div>
  <h2>Hội An — wandering the Ancient Town</h2>
  <div className="story">
    <div>
      On December 30, we checked out of our Airbnb in Huế and made our way to
      Hội An by limousine. After checking into our next Airbnb, we headed out to
      explore Hội An Ancient Town.
    </div>
    <div>
      Compared with the historical sights in Huế, Hội An was more about walking
      around and taking in the town. We wandered through the old streets, looked
      around the shops, and enjoyed having time to explore without a packed
      schedule.
    </div>
    <div>
      Hội An was one of the places we'd been looking forward to seeing, and it
      was nice to have a more relaxed change of pace after the sightseeing in
      Huế. The Ancient Town is known for its preserved architecture, lanterns,
      and riverside setting, so it's an easy place to spend time simply walking
      around rather than moving from one attraction to another.
    </div>
    <div>
      We also made time for food - including Bà Lê Well and a seafood dinner,
      along with a stop at Artbook for magnets and a postcard. I always like
      bringing home a few small souvenirs from a trip, and magnets are an easy
      way to remember the places we've visited without collecting too much
      stuff.
    </div>
    <div>
      The next morning, we checked out and continued toward Đà Nẵng. We didn't
      have a full extra day in Hội An, but even a short stay gave us time to
      explore the Ancient Town before moving on.
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

      of us were flying to Korea, with a long layover in Seoul before continuing
      home. It was a slightly unusual way to spend New Year's Eve, but it gave
      us one more chance to see more of the country before the trip ended.
    </div>
    <div>
      Our flight to Seoul departed Đà Nẵng late that night, marking the end of
      the Vietnam portion of the trip.

    </div>
