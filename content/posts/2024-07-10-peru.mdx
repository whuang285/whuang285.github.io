# Discovering Peru

From the ancient streets of Cusco to the sunny beaches of Máncora, Peru is a country of dramatic contrasts.

Our trip took us from the high-altitude Andes and the ruins of the Inca Empire to the warm northern coast. Along the way, we explored Machu Picchu, hiked through some of the highest landscapes in Peru, and spent a few relaxing days by the ocean.

This itinerary combines history, hiking, food, and plenty of time to slow down along the way.

---

# Cusco

Cusco was the first major stop on our trip and served as our base for exploring the surrounding region.

At more than 11,000 feet above sea level, altitude is the first thing you'll notice when arriving. The city itself is worth taking slowly, with narrow cobblestone streets, colonial architecture, colorful markets, and Inca ruins woven throughout the city.

## Where to Stay

We visited during Peru's winter, when temperatures could drop significantly at night.

Many accommodations don't have central heating. Instead, we were provided with a space heater, but it wasn't enough to warm the entire room and couldn't be left running overnight.

Temperatures during our stay ranged from around 20°F at night to 60°F during the day.

<InfoCard title="Choosing accommodations">
  <p>
    When booking accommodation in Cusco, look beyond the room itself. Some
    properties offer useful extras such as airport transfers and organized
    tours.
  </p>
  <p>
    We took advantage of these services and were able to book last-minute
    excursions to Rainbow Mountain and the Moray Salt Mines directly through our
    accommodation.
  </p>
</InfoCard>

# Day 1–2 — Arrival & Acclimating to Cusco

After landing in Lima and transferring to Cusco, we spent the first couple of days taking things slowly.

At 11,152 feet above sea level, Cusco is high enough that even normal activities can feel more strenuous than expected.

## Day 1 — Arrival

After checking in, we had a light lunch at one of Cusco's many cafés and explored the area around the city's main square.

### Plaza de Armas

**Plaza de Armas** is the heart of historic Cusco.

The square is surrounded by colonial buildings and two major churches:

- **Cusco Cathedral**
- **Iglesia de la Compañía de Jesús**

It's a good place to begin getting oriented with the city.

### Dinner

For dinner, we tried:

- **Chicha by Gastón Acurio** — a modern interpretation of traditional Peruvian cuisine.
- **Cicciolina** — a cozy restaurant offering tapas and Mediterranean-inspired dishes.

## Day 2 — Take It Slow

Rather than trying to pack the day with activities, we spent the second day exploring Cusco at a relaxed pace.

### San Blas

The bohemian **San Blas** neighborhood is filled with artisan workshops, galleries, cafés, and small shops.

Its narrow streets and steep hills also offer some great views over Cusco.

### San Pedro Market

We then visited **San Pedro Market**, where we browsed the local produce and food stalls.

It's a good place to try fresh fruit juices and traditional Andean street food.

### Nearby Inca Ruins

If you're feeling up to more walking, several Inca sites are located just outside the city.

Two options are:

- **Sacsayhuamán**
- **Tambomachay**

<TravelTip>
  <p>
    Don't feel like you need to fill your first two days with activities. Giving
    yourself time to adjust to Cusco's elevation makes it much easier to enjoy
    the rest of the trip.
  </p>
</TravelTip>

# Day 3 — Machu Picchu

No trip to Peru would be complete without visiting **Machu Picchu**.

We started early, traveling from Cusco to Ollantaytambo before taking the scenic train to Aguas Calientes. From there, a short bus ride brought us to the entrance of Machu Picchu.

The journey was long, but seeing the ancient citadel surrounded by the Andes made the early start worthwhile.

![Machu Picchu](/assets/img/2024-07-10-peru/machu-picchu.png)

## Choosing a Circuit

Machu Picchu uses designated circuits to manage visitor flow through the site.

When we visited, we wanted both the elevated views over the ruins and the opportunity to explore the structures more closely.

Our preferred **Circuit 2** was already fully booked, so we combined **Circuit 1** in the morning with **Circuit 3** in the afternoon.

### Circuit 1

Circuit 1 takes you above the main ruins and provides panoramic views over Machu Picchu and the surrounding mountains.

### Circuit 2

Circuit 2 provides a more comprehensive experience, combining elevated viewpoints with routes through the archaeological site.

### Circuit 3

Circuit 3 focuses more heavily on the lower portions and interior areas of the ruins.

<InfoCard title="Book Machu Picchu early">
  <p>
    Visitor numbers are limited and circuit availability can change. If you have
    a specific circuit in mind, especially one of the more popular options, book
    your tickets well in advance.
  </p>
  <p>
    We originally wanted Circuit 2, but it was already sold out. Combining
    Circuits 1 and 3 ended up being a great alternative because we got both the
    sweeping views and a closer look at the ruins.
  </p>
</InfoCard>

## Staying in the Sacred Valley

If you're looking for a less rushed Machu Picchu experience, consider staying in the **Sacred Valley** instead of returning to Cusco every night.

Staying near Ollantaytambo allows you to take the train directly to Aguas Calientes, eliminating the extra journey from Cusco.

The Sacred Valley is also at a lower elevation than Cusco, which can make it a more comfortable base while acclimating.

# Day 4–5 — Sacred Valley & High-Altitude Adventures

After Machu Picchu, there are several ways to spend the next couple of days.

## Sacred Valley

The **Sacred Valley** is filled with Inca archaeological sites, mountain scenery, traditional towns, and local markets.

### Ollantaytambo

Ollantaytambo is home to an impressive Inca archaeological complex and is also an important transportation hub for trips to Machu Picchu.

The town itself is worth exploring, with traditional stone streets and buildings dating back to the Inca period.

### Pisac

**Pisac** is known for its archaeological site and local market.

The combination of the ruins, surrounding mountains, and market makes it a worthwhile stop for anyone interested in the history and culture of the region.

---

## Rainbow Mountain

For a much more strenuous adventure, consider **Rainbow Mountain (Vinicunca)**.

The mountain reaches approximately 16,500 feet (5,000 meters), making altitude a major part of the challenge.

![Rainbow Mountain](/assets/img/2024-07-10-peru/rainbow-mountain.png)

The multicolored mountain slopes create an otherworldly landscape, but the altitude means this isn't a hike to underestimate.

<TravelTip>
  <p>
    Only attempt Rainbow Mountain once you've had enough time to acclimate to
    the altitude. Bring plenty of water, snacks, sun protection, and warm
    layers, and don't push yourself if you're feeling unwell.
  </p>
</TravelTip>

## More Time in Cusco

If you're feeling the effects of the altitude—or simply want a slower trip—use this day to explore more of Cusco instead.

There is plenty to see without taking on another high-altitude trek, and a slower day can be a welcome break after Machu Picchu.

---

# Day 5 — Moray & Travel to Lima

On our final day in the Cusco region, we visited **Moray** before heading back to Lima.

## Moray

Moray is an unusual Inca archaeological site consisting of large circular terraces carved into the landscape.

The terraces are thought to have been associated with agricultural experimentation, although the exact purpose of the site remains debated.

The surrounding Sacred Valley scenery makes the site just as memorable as the terraces themselves.

After visiting Moray, we returned to Cusco for an early-afternoon flight to Lima.

---

# Day 6–9 — Máncora

After several days of history, hiking, and high altitude, we headed to the coast.

**Máncora**, in northern Peru, is a laid-back beach town known for surfing, seafood, warm weather, and nightlife.

For us, the trip to Máncora was especially memorable because we were there for a wedding.

![Máncora](/assets/img/2024-07-10-peru/mancora.png)

## Beach Days

Most of our time in Máncora was intentionally unstructured.

We spent time relaxing by the beach, swimming, and enjoying the warm weather.

Despite visiting during Peru's winter, the ocean was surprisingly warm.

Nearby beaches such as **Vichayito** and **Las Pocitas** are also worth considering if you want to explore beyond central Máncora.

## Food

Máncora is a great place to eat seafood.

Look for local restaurants serving fresh **ceviche** and other Peruvian coastal dishes, particularly places close to the water.

## Having a Private Cook

One of the highlights of our stay was booking accommodation that included a private cook.

The food wasn't fancy, but it was authentic and delicious. More importantly, having someone prepare our meals turned out to be both easier and more cost-effective than buying groceries and cooking everything ourselves.

<InfoCard title="Consider a private cook">
  <p>
    If you're traveling with a group and staying somewhere with kitchen
    facilities, check whether the property offers a private cook.
  </p>
  <p>
    For us, it was a surprisingly convenient option and ended up costing less
    than we expected compared with shopping and preparing every meal ourselves.
  </p>
</InfoCard>

## Getting Around

Our accommodation was outside central Máncora, and we didn't have a car.

That meant relying on private taxis and tuk-tuks to get around.

Tuk-tuks were one of the more fun parts of getting around and were also an affordable option for shorter trips.

<TravelTip>
  <p>
    Carry small bills when taking tuk-tuks and agree on the price beforehand. We
    found that having cash in smaller denominations made transportation much
    easier.
  </p>
</TravelTip>

# Day 10–12 — Lima

After our weekend in Máncora, we returned to Lima for the final part of the trip.

Lima has a very different atmosphere from Cusco and Máncora, with a mix of historic architecture, modern neighborhoods, museums, restaurants, and Pacific Ocean views.

## Barranco

**Barranco** is one of Lima's most colorful neighborhoods, known for its street art, galleries, cafés, and creative atmosphere.

Walk through the neighborhood and visit **Puente de los Suspiros (Bridge of Sighs)** before exploring some of the surrounding streets.

## Historic Center

Lima's **historic center** is home to some of the city's most important colonial landmarks.

### Plaza de Armas

The main square is surrounded by historic buildings, including:

- **Lima Cathedral**
- **Government Palace**

The cathedral is particularly notable for its ornate colonial architecture.

### San Francisco Monastery

The **San Francisco Monastery** is another major historic attraction and is known for its catacombs.

## Food

Lima is one of the best places on the itinerary to focus on food.

### Maido

**Maido** offers a Peruvian-Japanese fusion menu and is a good option for a special dinner.

### Isolina

For something more casual and traditional, **Isolina** serves hearty Peruvian dishes.

Try classics such as **lomo saltado** or **ají de gallina**.

## Where to Stay

We recommend staying around **Miraflores**, particularly if it's your first time in Lima.

The neighborhood offers easy access to restaurants, shops, parks, and the coast.

### Malecón

The **Malecón** is a cliffside walkway overlooking the Pacific Ocean.

It's particularly enjoyable around sunset and is a great way to explore the city without needing to take transportation.

### Parque Kennedy

**Parque Kennedy** is another popular area to explore, with shops, restaurants, and local activity surrounding the park.

# Peru Travel Tips

## Take Altitude Seriously

Give yourself time to acclimate after arriving in Cusco.

Keep the first day or two relatively light rather than immediately scheduling strenuous hikes.

Coca tea and coca leaves are commonly used locally, although they shouldn't be considered a substitute for taking altitude symptoms seriously.

If you're feeling significantly unwell, don't push through a planned hike.

## Book Machu Picchu Early

Machu Picchu has limited visitor capacity and specific circuit availability.

If you know which circuit you want, book ahead rather than assuming you'll be able to purchase your preferred option when you arrive.

## Pack for Multiple Climates

Peru's geography means the weather can vary dramatically between destinations.

Cusco can be cold at night, particularly during winter, while Máncora is warm and beach-oriented.

Pack layers rather than relying on a single type of clothing for the entire trip.

<InfoCard title="What to pack">
  <p>
    <strong>Cusco:</strong> warm layers, a jacket, and clothing suitable for
    cold nights.
  </p>
  <p>
    <strong>High-altitude hikes:</strong> warm layers, sturdy shoes, sunscreen,
    water, and snacks.
  </p>
  <p>
    <strong>Máncora:</strong> swimwear, sunscreen, a hat, and lightweight
    clothing.
  </p>
</InfoCard>

## Carry Cash

Having access to cash is useful throughout Peru, particularly for smaller transportation providers and local businesses.

We found that having smaller bills was especially useful in Máncora when taking tuk-tuks.

A debit card with low or no ATM fees can also be useful when withdrawing local currency.

## Don't Underestimate the Sun

The combination of high elevation and strong sun in the Andes can make sun protection especially important.

Bring sunscreen, sunglasses, and a hat even on cooler days.

# Final Thoughts

Peru was one of those trips where every destination felt completely different from the last.

Cusco gave us history and architecture, Machu Picchu brought the iconic Inca landscapes, Rainbow Mountain pushed us into extreme elevations, and Máncora gave us several days to slow down by the ocean.

The biggest thing I'd recommend is **not trying to rush the altitude portion of the trip**. Give yourself time to adjust in Cusco before attempting Rainbow Mountain or other high-altitude excursions.

Then, after all that hiking and sightseeing, ending the trip with a few relaxed beach days in Máncora was the perfect contrast.
