# Southern Iceland Adventure

A week of waterfalls, black sand beaches, glaciers, volcanic landscapes, and geothermal pools.

My trip through southern Iceland was nothing short of breathtaking. Every day brought a completely different landscape—from towering waterfalls and black sand beaches to steaming lava fields and massive glaciers.

Rather than trying to see the entire country, we focused on southern Iceland and the Golden Circle. This gave us enough time for hikes, scenic drives, and spontaneous stops without spending the entire trip rushing from one destination to the next.

This guide covers our itinerary, what we did, what I would keep in mind when planning a similar trip, and what I packed for a late-September visit.

---

# When to Visit

## Late September

This itinerary was designed around a late-September trip.

One of the biggest advantages of visiting around this time is that winter hasn't fully arrived yet. Roads are generally easier to navigate, hiking trails are still accessible, and some highland destinations such as **Landmannalaugar** can still be reached.

The trade-off is daylight. We had only around 7–8 hours of daylight each day, but the shorter nights also gave us a better chance of seeing the **Northern Lights**.

Weather in Iceland can change dramatically throughout the day. We experienced a mix of sunshine, rain, and strong winds, sometimes within a matter of hours.

<InfoCard title="Late-September weather">
  <p>
    Expect temperatures around 40–55°F (5–13°C), along with frequent rain and
    wind.
  </p>
  <p>
    Waterproof layers and warm clothing are essential. Even when the forecast
    looks mild, the wind can make outdoor conditions feel considerably colder.
  </p>
</InfoCard>

# Itinerary

## Day 1 — Arrival & Golden Circle

We landed in Keflavík at 6:15 AM and picked up our rental car from **Blue Car Rental**. After stopping at **Krónan** for groceries, we started our drive toward the Golden Circle.

### Þingvellir National Park

Our first stop was Þingvellir National Park, where the North American and Eurasian tectonic plates meet.

The park has relatively easy walking trails through the rift valley and fissure zones. We also walked to **Öxarárfoss**, a waterfall surrounded by the dramatic landscape of the park.

### Kerið Crater

Next was Kerið, a volcanic crater with a striking blue lake surrounded by red volcanic rock and green vegetation.

It's a relatively quick stop, making it an easy addition to a Golden Circle road trip.

### Geysir & Gullfoss

We continued to the two major Golden Circle highlights:

- **Geysir** — the geothermal area where the famous Strokkur geyser erupts regularly.
- **Gullfoss** — a massive two-tiered waterfall and one of Iceland's most recognizable natural landmarks.

    <strong>Laugarvatn Fontana</strong>. You can experience geothermal bread
    baked using the area's geothermal heat and relax at the spa afterward.

</TravelTip>

## Day 2 — Highland Adventure: Landmannalaugar

Landmannalaugar was one of the most memorable parts of the trip.

This highland region is famous for colorful rhyolite mountains, lava fields, geothermal activity, and hiking trails. Access becomes much more difficult as winter approaches, so late September was already pushing the end of the hiking season.

When we arrived, the ground was muddy and slippery—a pretty good indication that winter conditions were approaching.

### Getting to Landmannalaugar

The drive took around three hours and was an adventure in itself, taking us deep into Iceland's volcanic highlands.

There are multiple routes to Landmannalaugar. Some involve river crossings and require an appropriate high-clearance vehicle. Routes and conditions can change throughout the season, so check current road conditions before setting out.

All routes involve **F-roads**, Iceland's rugged highland roads.

<InfoCard title="Driving the F-roads">
  <p>
    Do not assume that every rental vehicle can drive on F-roads. Check your
    rental agreement and make sure your vehicle is specifically authorized for
    highland travel.
  </p>
  <p>
    Some routes also involve river crossings, which require a suitable 4WD
    vehicle and experience with changing road conditions.
  </p>
</InfoCard>

### Hiking

We explored the **Laugahraun Lava Field** and hiked the **Bláhnjúkur–Brennisteinsalda loop**.

The terrain felt almost otherworldly, with black lava fields, steaming fumaroles, and brightly colored rhyolite mountains surrounding us.

The weather and muddy terrain made the hike challenging, but the scenery was absolutely worth it.

<TravelTip>
  <p>
    On the drive back, stop at <strong>Sigöldugljúfur Canyon</strong> if
    conditions and daylight allow. It's a beautiful detour through the Icelandic
    highlands.
  </p>
</TravelTip>

## Day 3 — South Coast

We continued along Iceland's south coast, visiting some of the region's most dramatic coastal and canyon landscapes.

### Dyrhólaey & Reynisfjara

**Dyrhólaey** is a dramatic coastal promontory famous for its arch-shaped rock formation and sweeping views of the black coastline.

Nearby **Reynisfjara Beach** is known for its black volcanic sand, towering sea stacks, and distinctive basalt columns.

The combination of the dark sand, crashing waves, and volcanic rock makes this one of the most visually striking stops on the south coast.

### Svartifoss

We then headed toward **Svartifoss**, a waterfall surrounded by distinctive black basalt columns.

The hike through the landscape was just as memorable as the waterfall itself.

### Fjaðrárgljúfur Canyon

Another highlight was **Fjaðrárgljúfur**, a dramatic canyon with walking trails and viewpoints overlooking the winding river below.

<TravelTip>
  <p>
    For a fun and casual lunch stop, check out the school bus café in{" "}
    <strong>Vík</strong>. It's a quirky alternative to the more conventional
    roadside stops.
  </p>
</TravelTip>

## Day 4 — Glacier Lagoon & Diamond Beach

Day four took us farther east toward the glaciers.

### Jökulsárlón Glacier Lagoon

**Jökulsárlón** is a glacial lagoon filled with icebergs that have broken away from the glacier.

Watching the ice drift through the lagoon was one of the highlights of the entire trip.

We also booked a **Vatnajökull ice cave tour**, which took us inside the glacier.

Ice cave tours typically begin later in the season, usually around October, but we were lucky enough to find an option in late September.

Because we visited early in the season, the glacier and cave still contained quite a bit of soot and ash. The ice wasn't as uniformly blue as the photos I had seen, but there were still incredible flashes of deep blue inside the glacier.

<InfoCard title="Ice cave season">
  <p>
    Ice cave conditions change throughout the year. Later in the season, the ice
    can become clearer and bluer, but availability and conditions depend heavily
    on weather and glacier conditions.
  </p>
  <p>
    Always book with an experienced operator and follow their guidance about
    current conditions.
  </p>
</InfoCard>

### Diamond Beach

Across the road from the lagoon is **Diamond Beach**, where chunks of glacial ice wash up on the black volcanic sand.

The contrast between the dark beach and sparkling pieces of ice makes the name feel immediately appropriate.

<TravelTip>
  <p>
    Bring a thermos filled with something hot. Standing along the glacier lagoon
    and beach can get very cold, and having a warm drink makes it much easier to
    linger and enjoy the scenery.
  </p>
</TravelTip>

## Day 5 — Waterfalls & Reykjavík

We started making our way back toward Reykjavík, stopping at several iconic waterfalls along the way.

### Skógafoss

**Skógafoss** is one of Iceland's most impressive waterfalls, with a massive curtain of water dropping from the cliffs.

It's an easy stop from the main road and one of the places where you can get surprisingly close to the waterfall.

### Seljalandsfoss

At **Seljalandsfoss**, you can walk behind the waterfall.

Be prepared to get wet—the spray can be substantial—but seeing the waterfall from behind is worth bringing waterproof clothing for.

### Reykjavík

After the waterfalls, we spent the afternoon exploring Reykjavík.

We visited:

- **Hallgrímskirkja** — Reykjavík's distinctive landmark church, with a tower offering panoramic views over the city.
- **Harpa Concert Hall** — a striking glass building on the waterfront and one of Reykjavík's most recognizable modern structures.

### Sky Lagoon

We finished the day at **Sky Lagoon**.

After several days of driving and hiking, spending the evening soaking in the geothermal water was the perfect way to slow down.

Sunset was especially nice, giving us a chance to relax while watching the sky change over the water.

## Day 6 — Snæfellsnes or Reykjavík

For the final full day, we had two options depending on how much driving we wanted to do.

### Option 1 — Snæfellsnes Peninsula

The **Snæfellsnes Peninsula** makes for a longer day trip but offers another collection of Iceland's dramatic coastal landscapes.

#### Búðakirkja

The small black **Búðakirkja** church is one of the peninsula's most recognizable sights.

It's surrounded by a stark landscape of lava fields and mountains, making the simple black building especially striking.

#### Arnarstapi Cliffs

We also visited the **Arnarstapi** coastline and walked along the dramatic cliffs overlooking the ocean.

The area has distinctive lava formations and sweeping coastal views.

<TravelTip>
  <p>
    After hiking around Arnarstapi, stop at <strong>Fjöruhúsið Café</strong> for
    coffee and a snack while enjoying the coastal scenery.
  </p>
</TravelTip>

### Option 2 — Reykjavík

If another long driving day isn't appealing, Reykjavík has plenty to fill a slower day.

#### Shopping

Spend the day walking through the city's streets, browsing local boutiques, galleries, bookstores, and independent shops.

#### Museums

For a cultural break, consider the **National Museum of Iceland** or the **Reykjavík Art Museum**.

#### Coffee

Finish the day at a local café such as **Mokka Kaffi** and take your time exploring the city.

# What to Pack

Late September requires packing for several different kinds of weather.

## Clothing

- **Warm layers** — thermal tops and bottoms, sweatshirts, lined leggings, and sweatpants.
- **Waterproof outer layers** — a warm snowboard jacket plus a waterproof rain jacket worked well for us.
- **Waterproof hiking boots** — essential for waterfalls, muddy trails, glaciers, and volcanic terrain.
- **Warm accessories** — gloves, scarves, and beanies are particularly useful on windy coastal days and glacier tours.

## Hiking & Outdoor Gear

- **Hand warmers** — especially useful for early-morning hikes and glacier activities.
- **Cooler bag** — helpful for keeping groceries and packed lunches cold during long drives.
- **Swimsuit** — essential if you plan to visit geothermal spas such as Sky Lagoon or Laugarvatn Fontana.

## Other Essentials

- **Power adapter** — Iceland uses Type C and Type F outlets.
- **Reusable water bottle** — Icelandic tap water is excellent and easy to refill.
- **Sandwich bags or aluminum foil** — useful for packing snacks and lunches for road trips and hikes.

<InfoCard title="Pack for the season">
  <p>
    This packing list is geared toward late September. A summer trip will
    require fewer warm layers, while winter travel requires substantially more
    insulation and preparation.
  </p>
  <p>
    Regardless of season, check the forecast and road conditions immediately
    before heading into remote areas.
  </p>
</InfoCard>

# Tips for the Road

## Stock Up on Groceries

Pick up groceries early, especially before heading into remote areas.

**Krónan** was useful for stocking up on food and drinks before long driving days. Having sandwiches and snacks in the car also meant we didn't have to plan every meal around restaurant stops.

A cooler bag makes this much easier.

## Book Popular Tours Early

Glacier hikes and ice cave tours can fill up quickly.

If you have a specific date in mind, book ahead—especially for activities that have a short seasonal window.

## Check Your Rental Car

If your itinerary includes Landmannalaugar or other highland destinations, confirm that your rental car is allowed on the relevant F-roads.

Don't rely solely on the vehicle being advertised as a 4WD. The rental agreement and road restrictions matter.

## Download Offline Maps

Cell service can be limited in remote parts of Iceland.

Download offline maps before leaving Reykjavík or another area with reliable connectivity. Having navigation available without cell service is particularly useful when driving through the highlands.

## Know How Gas Stations Work

One unexpected challenge for us was paying for gas.

Some Icelandic gas stations require a PIN for card payments, which can be problematic for U.S. credit cards. Even after requesting a PIN from my card issuer, I still couldn't get it to work at some stations.

**OB gas stations** were an exception for us because they didn't require a PIN.

We also received a discount card for OB purchases through **Blue Car Rental**, which made them particularly convenient.

<TravelTip>
  <p>
    Gas stations may place a temporary authorization hold of around $200 USD
    when you pay at the pump. Don't panic if you see a large pending charge—the
    hold should be released after the transaction is settled.
  </p>
</TravelTip>

## Prepare for Changing Weather

Iceland's weather can change quickly.

Even in late September, we experienced combinations of rain, wind, and sunshine within the same day. Waterproof layers, warm clothing, and sturdy footwear made a huge difference.

## Leave Room for the Unexpected

The biggest lesson from this trip was not to over-plan every hour.

Iceland is a place where the drive between two destinations can be just as memorable as the destination itself. Give yourself enough time to stop when you see an interesting landscape, take a break at a viewpoint, or simply sit somewhere and enjoy the scenery.

A packed itinerary might let you check off more locations, but leaving some breathing room makes the road trip much more enjoyable.

---

# Final Thoughts

Southern Iceland packs an incredible amount of variety into a relatively small area.

In one week, we went from volcanic craters and geothermal areas to colorful highlands, black sand beaches, waterfalls, glaciers, and geothermal lagoons.

The late-September timing worked particularly well for this itinerary. We still had access to many summer activities while getting longer nights and a chance to see the Northern Lights.

If I were planning the trip again, I would keep the focus on fewer regions rather than trying to drive around the entire country. The landscapes change constantly, and there is plenty to see without spending the entire trip behind the wheel.
